What Separates Elite from Average

5 Patterns All Top Sites Share

01

They Sell the Outcome, Not the Service

Top sites: "Come home to a Winter Wonderland." / "Make your home sparkle."

Average sites: "We install Christmas lights." / "Christmas light installation Fairfield County CT."

→ Sell the transformation and feeling, not the labor.

02

Mid-Page Lead Capture

Every top site places a quote form or CTA within the scrollable homepage body — not hidden in the footer or a separate contact page. The CTA comes to the user.

→ Don't make customers hunt for how to reach you.

03

A Documented 3–8 Step Process

Every top site shows their process: consultation → design → install → maintenance → removal → storage. This removes anxiety about "strangers on my roof."

→ Show how you work, not just what you do.

04

Layered Trust Stacking

Top sites combine: licensed + insured + years in business + warranty + named team + certification. Average sites say "insured" buried in a footer line.

→ Stack trust signals in a dedicated visible section.

05

Professional Dusk/Evening Photography

Every top site leads with blue-hour photography of illuminated homes. Dusk shots are dramatically more emotional and aspirational than daytime utility photos.

→ Blue-hour photography is non-negotiable for premium positioning.

Your Competitive Advantage

Market Gaps — Opportunities for Ornate

1

No one owns luxury residential end-to-end locally. Decorate With Lights is closest but is a NY franchise. A Fairfield County-native premium brand has a completely open lane.

2

No competitor positions above mid-market. Hang My Lights anchors the market at $499. The $2,000–$8,000+ estate market is completely unaddressed visually by anyone.

3

"Ornate" is the only name that signals craftsmanship. Every competitor is a landscaping company doing Christmas lighting on the side. Ornate Christmas Lighting is the only brand name built around the service and quality level itself.

4

FAQ/objection-handling gap. Only Summer Rain has a comprehensive FAQ. Most competitors leave common objections (who owns the lights? what if something breaks?) completely unanswered.

5

No before/after gallery. Every site shows "after" photos only. A properly executed before/after section would be immediately category-defining and emotionally powerful.

6

No live Google review widget on any homepage. Nobody prominently shows a star rating count in their hero section — a massive missed trust opportunity across the entire competitive set.

7

No video content. Zero competitors use hero video, drone footage, or time-lapse installation videos. A 30-second hero video of a crew transforming a Westport Colonial at dusk would be completely category-defining.
